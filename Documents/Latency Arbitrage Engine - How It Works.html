What it looks for

A fast advantage that fades

Latency arbitrage is a client profiting from prices that are briefly behind the market — a stale or slow quote — rather than from a view on where the market is going. Its signature is an advantage that appears within milliseconds of the fill and has disappeared a minute later.

Everything in the engine follows from that definition. A favourable move that holds is directional or informed flow, which is a different risk with a different treatment. Separating the two is the engine's main job, and the reason a client's score can be low even when they are consistently profitable.

Spec principle we keep to: a score is a risk signal, not an accusation. Repeated behaviour counts for more than a single event, and client profitability alone never proves anything.
Pipeline

Five stages, from trade record to measurement

01

Trades

Every closed trade from all six trading servers, held locally so a scan never depends on a live database.

MySQL → local warehouse · topped up every 15 min · 2,066,995 trades in the 7-day window
02

Millisecond fill times

Sub-second horizons need millisecond timestamps. MT5 records them natively; MT4 books to the whole second, so fills are matched to their Kafka trade events, which carry the execution stamp.

MT5 time_msc · MT4 Kafka tradeRecord.timeStamp (96% matched) · 98.2% of fills millisecond-stamped
03

Broker tick tape

The price the client actually dealt on, taken from the trade server's own tick table — bid, ask and the age of the quote at the moment of the fill.

Server ticks tables · 97.3% of trades covered through the 60-second horizon
04

Independent reference market

A second, unrelated venue — a Vantage Raw ECN feed in its own read-only terminal. Its clock offset is re-derived each day against our tape, and the standing price difference between the two brokers is removed, so what remains is a genuine gap rather than a constant.

6.47M reference ticks · covers 100% of qualifying trades
05

Markouts

Each qualifying trade is marked out at the seven horizons the spec fixes, on both feeds. The reference markout uses the executable opposite side — a buy exits at the reference bid, a sell at the ask.

100 / 200 / 300 / 500 ms · 1 s · 5 s · 60 s
Measurement

Seven horizons, and one rule about quote age

The shape of the curve carries the information, not any single number.

Quote throttling. Our quote feed is throttled at 200 ms, so a quote up to 200 ms old is normal behaviour and cannot count as evidence. Stale-price evidence starts only beyond 200 ms plus a 100 ms tolerance, and only on millisecond-stamped fills. The 100 ms markout stays a measurement; it is never treated as proof of a stale quote.
Per trade

Latency event, or directional trade?

A trade has to qualify before it is judged at all: held no longer than 120 seconds and profitable by at least $1. A qualifying trade becomes a latency event when its early markout clears the threshold for its horizon and that advantage has faded by at least half at 60 seconds. If the advantage holds or grows instead, the trade is recorded as directional and never counts toward a latency alert.

Early thresholds run from 0.75 bps at 100 ms to 1.9 bps at 1 s — each set just above the 99th percentile of normal flow at that horizon, so ordinary market movement cannot trigger one.

Safeguards

When the engine holds itself back

A verdict is capped at Monitor — never escalated automatically — when any of these apply:

  • Low confidence, or a minimum the desk has set (hit rate, event success, profit concentration) is not met.
  • The pattern repeats across unrelated accounts at the same price moment. 81 such moments in this window: several clients profiting on the same gold price within two seconds. That points at pricing, not at clients.
  • The client's events sit inside a flagged feed condition — our own feed created the opportunity.
Feed before client. The engine measures our own aggregated feed against the reference: how often our price sat behind it, how often that turned into a client gain, how many clients, and the P&L involved. In this window 17 feed-and-symbol combinations show any stale fills — gold on MT4 is the largest at 165 fills, 0.3% of executions — and none reaches the level where the feed is treated as the cause. If one did, the fix would be the feed, not the client.

Evidence

What we can put in front of a client

Any flagged order opens a one-page evidence pack: the trade as booked to the millisecond, our bid and ask against the reference bid and ask at the fill and at each horizon, the markout in basis points and dollars, which thresholds it cleared, the quote age, entry and exit marked on the curve, and the classification in plain words. Sources and method are printed on it, and it exports to PDF or CSV.

The honest reading: no proven latency arbitrage in the current book. The clients on the list trade fast and win early more often than normal flow, usually on one symbol, but their fills are not on stale prices, the independent market does not confirm them as latency, and the money involved is small. Most of their edge persists past 60 seconds, which makes it directional flow — a real risk, but one for the toxic-flow treatment rather than this engine.

That result is worth presenting as a finding in itself: it says our pricing is not being picked off, and it is backed by an independent venue rather than by our own feed.

Operations & limits

What runs, and what we still cannot see

  • Data top-up every 15 minutes, full rescan every 20 minutes; the tab shows when the last scan ran and when the next is due.
  • Shadow mode is on. Every verdict is logged, nothing is applied to a client automatically.
  • One reference venue. A second would harden L1 and L4 considerably.
  • No constituent feed IDs. Our tick data names one aggregated feed, so we can say our price was behind the reference, but not which liquidity provider caused it. Getting per-feed data would complete the spec's root-cause layer and the L6 and L8 profiles.
  • Alert minimums are still at zero pending calibration; confidence is the only gate currently enforced.
