The question

Every backtest elsewhere on this site assumes the copy book mirrors the client's whole round trip — in at their entry, out at their exit. That is a real policy, but it inherits the client's worst habit: holding losers. An independent exit cannot make a bad signal good; it can stop one trade destroying a month.

Scope: measured only on the {{ '{:,}'.format(exits.trades) }} trades the model would actually copy ({{ exits.rule }}, {{ '{:.2%}'.format(exits.share_of_flow) }} of flow). Evaluating exits across all client flow would answer a question the desk never asks — it only chooses an exit for trades it has taken on.

{% if not exits.path_aware %}

These stop and target results are not evidence

The warehouse stores entry and exit, not the path between them, so there is no intrabar high or low. A stop is therefore applied to the trade's realised outcome — and a stop applied that way can only ever increase total profit. It truncates losses and touches nothing else. Every changed trade is, by construction, a loss made smaller.

So a stop row showing "+$5.2M vs mirror" is measuring the arithmetic of truncation, not the value of the policy. The real cost of stops — trades that dipped through the level and would have recovered — is invisible here and is exactly what makes stops expensive in practice. Do not set stops from this table. Settling it needs the minute-bar replay in tick_bars.excursions: once each trade carries its worst adverse and best favourable excursion, this page evaluates against what price actually did and the warning disappears. The mirror row and the tail-loss column remain informative.

The `time` rows are labelled APPROXIMATION for a reason: do not trust them. A time exit here credits the trade with its realised P&L scaled linearly by the share of the holding period elapsed — it never looks at the price path, because minute bars say where price went, not what the position was worth at each moment. P&L does not accrue linearly (most of these trades' P&L arrives in bursts), so "close after 1h and keep 1h/hold of the final P&L" manufactures a smooth curve with almost no drawdown. That is why the top rows look remarkable and differ only in their Hold column — they are the same arithmetic at 1h, 4h and 24h. Settling time exits honestly needs a mark-to-market replay against the bars, which the data supports but this table does not yet do. The stop / target / bracket rows ARE path-aware and are trustworthy.

Tail loss is the share of all losses coming from the worst 1% of trades — the number a stop exists to move. A policy that improves total P&L without cutting the tail has probably just trimmed ordinary losers, and will not protect against the trade that matters.
