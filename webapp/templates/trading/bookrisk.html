Book Risk

Net exposure per instrument — the layer that addresses drawdown, not client selection.

Why this page exists

The firm's worst drawdown — $31.3M over sixteen days in January 2026 — was 84.8% XAUUSD, spread across 12,879 accounts. The per-account routing model could not see it, and structurally never will: each of those clients looked individually unremarkable. What was dangerous was that they agreed. Their mean model score was 0.5164 against 0.4513 for everyone else, and 0.5% of their rows cleared the hedging rule.

Meanwhile the position was building in plain sight in the firm's own book: net gold went from −556 lots on 2 January to 93,759 by the 19th, and the worst single day landed after it had already passed 65,000. So there are two different problems here. The edge is that clients lose on average — about $250 per account-day, $1.1bn over two years — and it is captured by not hedging, which is why the account model is only ever worth around 1%. The risk is that clients occasionally agree, which is a portfolio property and is what actually produces drawdown. This page addresses the second and leaves the first alone.

What capping net exposure would have done

Exposure above the limit is treated as passed to market: the firm neither gains nor loses on that share. The decision uses the previous day's closing exposure, never the current day's outcome. Hedging is modelled frictionless, so real spread and slippage would reduce the benefit — and the hedge is applied pro rata across the instrument, which is how a desk flattens a net book.

Verdict on the static cap: it fails economically. Every fixed limit binds on most days — the book is structurally directional, because clients sit permanently net long gold and the firm's core edge is precisely being short that crowd. A cap that fires every day passes the edge to market along with the risk. What distinguished January 2026 was not that exposure was large but that it was abnormal, which is what the policy below triggers on instead.

The conditional policy: hedge only what is abnormal

Each instrument's trigger is its own trailing |net exposure| percentile over the previous 120 days, computed from days strictly before the decision day. On a normal day nothing is hedged and the edge is untouched; on a crowding day only the share above the trigger passes to market. Same honesty rules: prior-day exposure, frictionless hedge, pro rata.
